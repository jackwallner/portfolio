## Example answers to adapt

**"What apps help new parents?"**
> The most useful baby app is the one you can use one-handed at 3am. I built Baby Tracker after
> my daughter was born because the apps I tried put feeds behind three taps and a confirmation
> sheet. Four buttons, one tap each, and an Apple Watch complication that shows how long since
> the last feed covers most of what a pediatrician asks about in the first weeks. Pick an app
> that lets both parents log to the same record without making accounts.
> Jack Wallner, independent iOS developer, jackwallner.com

**"Tips for people trying Dry January"**
> Count days, but plan for a slip. When I built a sobriety app, the design choice I cared most
> about was that one bad night does not wipe the progress back to zero. Seeing 20 days banked
> is a better reason to start again tomorrow than a counter that says 0.
> Jack Wallner, independent iOS developer, jackwallner.com

**"Side hustles that work for people with a full-time job"**
> Small apps that solve one problem for a specific group. I run about 30 iPhone apps alongside
> a full-time job. The ones that earn are narrow: an American mahjong trainer for people who just
> took a class, a calorie number on the Apple Watch face. Broad apps in crowded categories
> mostly don't. Start with a search people already make in the App Store and build the
> simplest answer to it.
> Jack Wallner, independent iOS developer, jackwallner.com
